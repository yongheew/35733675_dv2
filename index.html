Smoking in Malaysia

A data story on health burden, smoking behaviour, public perception, and the tobacco trade.

Section 1

The Health Burden of Smoking

Tobacco use is one of Malaysia's leading preventable causes of death. Between 1990 and 2023, smoking-related deaths have risen steadily — and so have deaths from secondhand smoke, showing that tobacco harm reaches far beyond the individual smoker.

Smoking-related deaths Secondhand smoke deaths
Key insight: The 2020 dip aligns with COVID-19 disruption and competing mortality, while 2022 shows a rebound as Malaysia moved into the endemic phase and activity normalised. The 1999 increase appears in the IHME modelled estimates for both active and secondhand-smoke deaths, so it is safest to label it as a modelled rise rather than a confirmed policy shock.

The diseases behind these deaths are not evenly spread. Some diseases kill more than they disable; others cripple without killing. Comparing death rank with DALY rank reveals which diseases carry the heaviest hidden burden.

Key insight: Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease is written in full to avoid abbreviation confusion. Stroke disables more than it kills — its DALY rank is higher than its death rank. Diabetes mellitus is even more striking: it ranks 6th in deaths but causes far more years of healthy life lost, making it a silent epidemic among smokers.
Section 2

Smoking Behaviour

Why has smoking not declined faster in Malaysia? One answer lies in tax policy. Countries that tax cigarettes heavily tend to see lower consumption — but Malaysia sits in a troubling middle ground: moderate taxes, yet persistently high consumption compared to regional peers.

Malaysia's position: At 57% tax share and 15 cigarettes per smoker per day, Malaysia has higher taxes than the Philippines or Indonesia — yet consumption remains well above the regional average. Thailand, with 70% tax, achieves notably lower consumption.

The regional picture shows how different Southeast Asian countries have changed over time. Use the slider to compare cigarette consumption per smoker across the region from 1980 to 2012.

Key insight: Malaysia remains in the middle of Southeast Asia: daily cigarette consumption is lower than the region’s highest-consuming countries but still above several neighbours, suggesting that regional tobacco control progress is uneven.
Section 3

Who Smokes in Malaysia?

Smoking in Malaysia is not evenly distributed. Gender, ethnicity, age, and education level all shape who smokes. Use the dropdown to explore how each demographic group compares between ever-smokers and non-smokers.

Gender gap: Over 98% of ever-smokers in Malaysia are male — one of the starkest gender disparities in smoking rates globally. Malay ethnicity and lower education levels are also associated with higher smoking rates.

E-cigarette use tells a separate story. Rather than spreading broadly, vaping remains concentrated among those who already smoke — suggesting it functions as an alternative rather than a gateway to smoking.

Key insight: E-cigarette use is concentrated among ever-smokers, supporting the interpretation that vaping is more common as a substitute or complement to smoking than as a behaviour evenly adopted across all groups.
Section 4

Perception on E-Cigarettes

How smokers and non-smokers view e-cigarettes differs substantially. Explore four perception categories — harm, regulations, reasons to use, and quitting — using the selector below.

Key insight: Smokers and non-smokers diverge most on whether e-cigarettes are useful for quitting and how harmful they are, showing that personal smoking experience shapes risk perception.

The diverging chart below sharpens the picture. Smokers are consistently more likely to agree that e-cigarettes are helpful — click the legend to highlight either group.

Key insight: Agreement is generally stronger among ever-smokers, especially on statements that frame e-cigarettes as helpful or acceptable. This makes the chart useful for comparing attitude direction, not just response size.

Despite widespread e-cigarette use among smokers, awareness of basic facts remains surprisingly low. Only 56% of ever-smokers correctly knew that e-cigarettes contain nicotine.

Key insight: The donut charts show that awareness is incomplete even among ever-smokers. Only the centre value represents the share answering “Yes”; the remaining segments show uncertainty, incorrect beliefs, or alternative answers, highlighting gaps in basic e-cigarette knowledge.
Section 5

Tobacco Trade Flows

Malaysia is both a significant exporter of manufactured tobacco products and an importer of raw tobacco leaf. Use the controls to explore where Malaysian tobacco goes and where raw tobacco comes from — select a zoom region for detail.

Key insight: Malaysia’s trade network extends beyond Southeast Asia. The added Europe, North America, and World zoom levels reveal long-distance partners while graticules and ocean fill make the map read as a complete world-map idiom.

Rankings reveal a deeper story. Singapore has dominated as Malaysia's top export destination since 2012, but Indonesia — once the largest market — has steadily fallen. Click the legend to trace a single country's trajectory.

Behind the volumes, a structural shift has occurred. Malaysia was a net exporter of finished tobacco products until 2017, when the trade balance flipped — exports collapsed as domestic taxes rose and regional markets tightened.

Tipping point (2017): For the first time, Malaysia became a net importer of finished tobacco products — a sign that domestic manufacturing is declining even as raw tobacco imports remain high.
